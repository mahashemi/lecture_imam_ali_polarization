\section{The Elixir of Love}

\begin{frame}{Love Is the Real Philosopher's Stone}
\footnotesize
\bigquote{Love, absolutely, is the ``elixir''\ldots\ which changes one nature into another.}{Mutahhari}
{\footnotesize Copper into gold. Bitterness into sweetness.}
\bigquote{Love is the power of glory, it makes the coward courageous.}{Mutahhari}
\examplenote{The hen}{Timid and fleeing when alone -- but with chicks to defend, she lowers her wings, attacks instead of fleeing.}
\bigquote{In the spirit, the effect of love is\ldots\ development and thriving; in the body\ldots\ melting and decomposition.}{Mutahhari}
{\footnotesize Destroys the body. Perfects the spirit -- removes envy, avarice, cowardice, laziness, self-admiration.}
\delivernote{Share, in a sentence: a time love (for a person, cause, or craft) made you braver or more disciplined than usual?}

\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Love's Transformations --- Mutahhari's Own Examples}

\end{frame}

% ---------------- Q&A ----------------
\qaframe{The Elixir of Love}{%
  \qa{Where does the term ``elixir'' for love come from, and what did the alchemists originally mean by it?}
     {Persian poets borrowed it from the alchemists, who believed in a mythical substance -- also called the philosopher's stone -- capable of transmuting one material into another. The poets said love is the \emph{real} elixir: people are like mines, and love is what refines them.}
  \qa{Describe the hen example -- what does it illustrate about love's effect on courage?}
     {Alone, a hen is timid, fleeing even from a child. Once she has chicks to defend, love transforms her: wings lowered for defence, clucking louder, attacking rather than fleeing -- courage born from love, not personality.}
  \qa{What is the difference between love's effect on the body versus its effect on the spirit?}
     {In the body, love's effects can be destructive -- pallor, sleeplessness, disorder in the digestive and nervous systems. In the spirit, it is purifying -- it develops and perfects, turning bitterness sweet and copper to gold.}
  \qa{Name at least three negative qualities the text says love purifies from the spirit.}
     {Envy, avarice, cowardice, laziness, conceitedness, self-admiration, grudges, and malevolence.}
}
