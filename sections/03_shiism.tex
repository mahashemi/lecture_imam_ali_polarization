\section{Shi'ism, the Religion of Love}

\begin{frame}{Love That Survives the Sword}
\bigquote{One of the greatest marks of distinction of Shi'ism\ldots\ is that its foundation and its bedrock is love.}{Mutahhari}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item ``'Ali and his party will be the triumphant ones.'' --- the Prophet (s)
  \item ``The believer will never be your enemy, the hypocrite will never love you.''
\end{itemize}
\bigquote{'Ali is the standard and criterion for assaying human nature and temperaments.}{Mutahhari}
\examplenote{The man whose hand was cut off}{Punished justly by 'Ali, he still extols him at length to the Kharijite Ibn al-Kawwa': ``Why should I not extol him, now that his friendship is mixed with flesh and blood?''}
\delivernote{Guess before the reveal: did the man curse 'Ali, stay silent, or praise him?}

\end{frame}

% ---------------- Q&A ----------------
\qaframe{Shi'ism, the Religion of Love}{%
  \qa{What Prophetic statement does Mutahhari use to root Shi'ism in the principle of love?}
     {``'Ali and his party (Shi'ah) will be the triumphant ones'' -- from this he argues Shi'ism's foundation and bedrock is love and devotion to 'Ali.}
  \qa{According to 'Ali's own words, why will a hypocrite never love him no matter what he does for them?}
     {He said that striking a true believer with his sword would never turn that believer into his enemy, and pouring the world's wealth on a hypocrite's head would never make the hypocrite love him -- because, as the Prophet decreed, the believer will never be 'Ali's enemy and the hypocrite will never love him.}
  \qa{What does the story of the man whose hand was cut off demonstrate about real love for 'Ali?}
     {That such love is rooted in truth and justice, not personal comfort or benefit -- he was justly punished, yet still praised 'Ali at length because he recognised the punishment as rightly established by God.}
  \qa{In what sense does Mutahhari describe 'Ali as a ``standard'' or ``criterion'' for human nature?}
     {A sound, pure nature will not turn against 'Ali even under his sword, while a diseased nature will show him no attachment even after receiving great favours -- because 'Ali is nothing but the embodiment of truth itself.}
}
