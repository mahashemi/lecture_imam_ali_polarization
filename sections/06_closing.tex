\section{Closing}

\begin{frame}{The Well That Never Runs Dry}
\bigquote{He is like the laws of nature which remain unchanged by time --- a well-spring which is never dry, but which rather increases day by day.}{Mutahhari, on 'Ali}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=5pt]
  \item Love and hate: not a contradiction --- a completed faith
  \item 'Ali: love strong enough to outlive centuries
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

% ---------------- Q&A (full-session review) ----------------
\qaframe{Full Session Review}{%
  \qa{What makes 'Ali's power of attraction ``three-dimensional'' rather than one- or two-dimensional?}
     {It spread widely across society, has lasted for centuries rather than fading, and penetrates to the depth of the human heart -- a combination Mutahhari says is unique to the chain of the prophets.}
  \qa{How does the story of the punished man (fingers cut off) prove that love for 'Ali isn't self-interested?}
     {He had every material reason to resent 'Ali, yet praised him at length -- showing his devotion was rooted in recognising truth and justice, not in personal comfort or favour.}
  \qa{What is the difference between love's effect on the body and its effect on the spirit?}
     {In the body it can be destructive (pallor, sleeplessness, disorder); in the spirit it is purifying and perfecting, removing envy, cowardice, laziness and self-admiration.}
  \qa{What does it mean to ``expand the self'' rather than eliminate self-love?}
     {Legitimate self-regard is not erased -- instead, the boundary of ``self'' is pushed outward to include other people, and ideally all of creation, redirecting one's drives and affections beyond the self.}
  \qa{Why does Mutahhari say 'Ali ``became more alive'' after his martyrdom rather than being forgotten?}
     {Because his connection to people was not material but spiritual -- unlike other historical champions who fade from memory, 'Ali's influence and remembrance have only grown across the centuries.}
}
