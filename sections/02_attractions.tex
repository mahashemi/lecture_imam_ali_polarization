\begin{frame}{A Call That Never Stopped Ringing}
\footnotesize
\bigquote{'Ali's power of attraction was [three-dimensional]\ldots\ not limited to one or two centuries.}{Mutahhari}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item One-dimensional $\rightarrow$ fades. 
  \item Two-dimensional $\rightarrow$ lasts.
  \item Three-dimensional $\rightarrow$ reaches the depth of the heart --- the mark of a prophet's call
\end{itemize}

\delivernote{Guess: how many years after 'Ali's death did someone die for praising him publicly? (Twenty.)}

\end{frame}

% ---------------- Q&A ----------------
\qaframe{Powerful Attractions}{%
  \qa{What are the three ``dimensions'' of a call to humanity, and which does 'Ali's attraction belong to?}
     {One-dimensional calls spread widely, then fade. Two-dimensional calls spread widely \emph{and} last through time. Three-dimensional calls spread widely, last through centuries, \emph{and} penetrate the depth of the human heart -- unique to the chain of the prophets. 'Ali's attraction belongs to this third, three-dimensional category.}
  \qa{How long after 'Ali's death did Maytham at-Tammar speak from his own crucifixion in praise of him?}
     {Twenty years, under brutal Umayyad repression -- he was eventually gagged and executed for it.}
  \qa{What did Ibn as-Sikkit say to Caliph al-Mutawakkil, and what did it cost him?}
     {Asked whether his own sons were dearer to him than the caliph's, he said even 'Ali's slave Qanbar was dearer to him than the caliph and his sons -- and had his tongue cut out for the answer.}
  \qa{What does Mutahhari mean when he says 'Ali ``became more alive'' after his death?}
     {Unlike other historical champions of speech, knowledge, or the battlefield -- who are eventually forgotten -- 'Ali's memory and influence only grew stronger after his martyrdom, because his link to people was not a material one but a bond of the spirit.}
  \qa{Where do figures like Satan, Hitler, Rumi, Iqbal, Allama Majlisi, or Sheikh al-Tusi fit in this 1D/2D/3D model?}
     {The model tracks reach and duration, not moral direction -- so it doesn't sort cleanly on its own. Satan and Hitler arguably have deep, lasting pull (closer to 2D or even 3D in reach) but it's destructive, not the constructive spiritual depth Mutahhari means -- suggesting the model needs a direction axis, not just a depth axis. Rumi and Iqbal genuinely move hearts across centuries (3D-like), but they're not founders of a call -- they're transmitting and amplifying one (Islam, 'Ali's own example), which may be a different category from originating a call. Allama Majlisi and Sheikh al-Tusi have centuries of real scholarly influence within the tradition, but that's closer to 2D (enduring, intellectual) than the heart-level authority of 3D.}
  \qa{Could Intensity (cost of sacrifice) or Polarity (attraction + repulsion together) sharpen this model further?}
     {Possibly -- both are real gaps in the 1D/2D/3D framework as stated. Intensity would capture what reach and duration alone miss: Maytham lost his life, Ibn as-Sikkit lost his tongue -- the model doesn't distinguish a costly, demanding pull from an easy, comfortable one. Polarity ties back to this session's very first framework (the Four Kinds of People): real conviction always creates enemies too, so a pull that attracts everyone and repels no one (Type 2, often hypocrisy) shouldn't score the same as 'Ali's kind of pull. Both are extensions beyond Mutahhari's own text, worth flagging as open questions for discussion rather than settled answers.}
